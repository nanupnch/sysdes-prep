## How it works {#how}

- **Predict before you read.** Each module opens with a question. A wrong guess still makes the answer easier to remember (the pretesting effect).
- **Answer before you reveal.** Answers stay hidden until you've written yours and said how sure you are. Recalling builds memory far better than rereading (the testing effect).
- **Fix confident mistakes first.** Misses you were sure about go to the top of the error log on the Progress page. They're the likeliest to surface in the room, and the easiest to fix once seen (the hypercorrection effect).
- **Speak and draw in the mocks.** The interview is spoken and drawn, so practise out loud, on paper, against the clock.

Topics carry stars: ★★★ must be known at senior depth, ★★ are likely follow-ups, ★ is worth knowing exists. Behind schedule? Skip ★, skim ★★, and never skip a check or a mock. Bring paper, a pen and a phone that records audio.

## Choose a schedule {#schedule}

Spreading study across days beats cramming it into one (the spacing effect), so take the longest schedule your date allows:

| Schedule | When | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| **3 days × 2 hours** (best) | 4 or more days away | One Part a day. Days 2 and 3 open with 10 minutes of error-log re-tests |
| **2 days × 3 hours** | 2–3 days away | Day 1: Part 1, then 2.1–2.5. Day 2: a warm-up, the rest of Part 2 with Mock A, then Part 3 |
| **1 day × 6 hours** | Tomorrow | Three 2-hour blocks with 15-minute breaks, then sleep. In the morning, re-test the error log |

Rest for 5–10 minutes every 45–60 minutes, at a check.
